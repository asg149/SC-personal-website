Examination of Artificial Neural Networks and their Importance

AI and its usage has been growing rapidly in recent years, sparking mixed reactions. Some embrace the new technology enthusiastically, recognizing its potential to revolutionize industries and improve quality of life, while others are more wary of the concept, worrying that humanity may lose control over AI allowing it to take over, still others see possible ethical issues within AI. This rapid growth forces the average person to try to understand AI’s strengths, weaknesses and dangers, as it becomes more directly integrated into our daily lives. Within this examination of AI it is crucial to set aside biases such as fear or excitement and evaluate AI technologies with a rational perspective. By examining the inner workings of artificial neural networks (ANNs) as well as their applications, it becomes clear that ANNs are designed to complement human intelligence rather than replace it. These networks can serve as tools to enhance human abilities in certain areas. It is also clear that despite the societal and ethical challenges posed by these programs they can contribute in a positive way to society. Looking at both the strengths and weaknesses of AI allows a clearer image to be drawn of where it belongs within society and how to safely and effectively implement it.

The first necessary step to understanding the place of these ANNs within human society is to gain a solid understanding of the networks themselves. Abstracting them down to their core, these networks are applied linear algebra and calculus modeled to imitate the human brain. However, this is only the core idea. These are not actual brains, networks like these can excel in places where humans lack the specified skills required and fail in places where the creative thought of the human brain is required. This becomes apparent when you start to look deeper than the basic concept. One example of this is gradient descent: a type of algorithm that affects the “learning rate” of an ANN. As described by IBM, gradient descent “trains machine learning models by minimizing errors between predicted and actual results.” To understand it, think of gradient descent as a ball on hilly terrain. Due to the slope of the hills the ball will eventually roll down to the lowest point. When the hill is steeper, the ball will move faster and it will slow down at the bottom of the hill where the slope is more mellow. This is the basis of gradient descent. It is basically a high dimension version of a ball rolling down hills. This is not the way that the human brain works. Similarly an algorithm called back propagation uses calculus to adjust each neuron’s effect on the final output. Even though the basic structure of ANNs take inspiration from the human brain, they are not replicas and are merely applied mathematical concepts that imitate learning.

Artificial neural networks hold a unique role in society, not as replacements for humans but as tools designed to help humans in the same way as a calculator or smartphone. The strength of these networks is in their ability to recognize patterns in vast amounts of data, where humans lack the speed or attention to detail required. For example, during brain surgeries, neural networks are used to detect seizures by analyzing brainwave activity of patients. According to a study published in Frontiers in Neurology, deep learning models can identify irregularities that suggest seizures with over 90% accuracy, offering important support for surgeons in life-saving procedures. This capability is far greater than even the most highly skilled human experts can achieve in such time-sensitive situations. Similarly, in the medical field, ANNs have been used for early cancer detection by analyzing medical images. Research demonstrated that neural networks could identify breast cancer, with models achieving sensitivity rates of up to 94% in detecting tumors.

However, these systems rely entirely on the mathematical processes described earlier. They operate solely based on their training data, unlike the human brain. The ANNs do what they are trained to do well but they are unable to do anything else. For example, while neural networks can classify millions of images and patterns they cannot think beyond their training and they are not even sure what they are doing they are just maximizing efficiency for some reward function. This is what defines their role as tools that excel in areas of specialization, not as entities that can replace the human mind.

Beyond their technical applications, the impacts that artificial neural networks have on society are important as well. ANNs have benefitted industries by optimizing efficiency, reducing costs, and advancing new and important innovations. For example, within healthcare, neural networks have become important tools that can help doctors predict coming issues for patients before any symptoms appear. One article found that these models of ANNs reduced hospital readmission rates to a significant degree This saves both lives and resources. Similarly, in finance, fraud detection systems built on neural networks have significantly improved accuracy. According to one report, machine learning models have the ability to catch up to 95% of credit card fraud cases, far surpassing traditional methods.

However, this growing influence also raises ethical concerns. Biases in training data can lead to discriminatory outcomes. For example, in the case of Amazon’s hiring algorithm an investigation revealed that the company’s AI recruiting tool discriminated against female applicants due to biased training data. Because of issues such as this and the lack of understanding most people have for AI there is general distrust towards it. This distrust is backed in cases in criminal justice, where systems have been found to have racial bias. As these technologies continue to grow, it is critical that they are not simply accepted as fact and that there is a general understanding of how they work. A balance must be found where humans reap the benefits of AI without blindly trusting that they know best. This balance will allow us to use AI for good by making sure that the training data is correct and unbiased.

In conclusion, artificial neural networks have quickly become tools that society relies on. They have become capable of complementing human abilities and solving specific but complex problems. These systems are no more than applied mathematical principles and are designed to recognize patterns in large sets of data. They can be helpful in fields like healthcare and finance, where their precision and efficiency is more than is possible for humans. However, as we rely more on these systems, it’s crucial to address their limitations, such as biases in training data and the lack of transparency for users. By understanding their strengths, applications, and limitations, we can appreciate ANNs for what they truly are: mathematical algorithms that humans can use for certain tasks. To ensure that these technologies have a positive impact on humanity, we must promote ethical usage, understanding, and continuous monitoring of the networks. Approaching AI with this perspective can allow humans to reap its benefits while minimizing its risks, ensuring that artificial neural networks help society rather than hindering it.
